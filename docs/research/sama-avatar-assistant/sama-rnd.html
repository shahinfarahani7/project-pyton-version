R&D report · Draft 1 · 7 October 2026

A Sama-style AI avatar
for your reservation system

What Qatar Airways built, how it works, and how to build a comparable assistant without owning a powerful GPU — with requirements, architecture options, costs and a phase-by-phase implementation guide.

Finding

Sama is not one model. It is a conversation AI + knowledge base + booking tools + voice + an animated face, joined into one pipeline.

No-GPU answer

Rent the heavy parts as APIs (language model, speech, avatar video) or render a 3D avatar in the user's browser. Your server only orchestrates — a normal CPU machine is enough.

Recommendation

Build in order: text assistant → safe booking actions → voice → avatar. The avatar is the last 20%, not the first step.

Scope and honesty notes

The Qatar Airways Sama page blocked automated access, so this report relies on Qatar Airways' newsroom, UneeQ's case study and press releases, NVIDIA's developer blog and vendor documentation (sources on the last page). Qatar Airways and UneeQ have not published the language model they use. Prices are third-party summaries dated September 2026 and must be re-checked on vendor pages before you commit. Your reservation system's API was not inspected; integration steps are written generically.

01 · Research

What Qatar Airways' Sama actually is

Sama ("sky" in Arabic) is Qatar Airways' AI "digital cabin crew": a photorealistic digital human that talks with customers by voice or chat, answers travel questions and can help book flights.

AspectWhat is publicly stated
BuilderDeveloped with UneeQ (digital-human company). Sama 2.0 launched March 2024 at ITB Berlin; Sama 1.0 dated from 2022.
Face and animationUneeQ Synanim: real-time facial animation, gestures and emotion; NVIDIA Audio2Face integrated for lip-sync. UneeQ states sub-1-second synchronised speech and expression.
ConversationGenerative AI with retrieval (RAG) over Qatar Airways content — "1,000 topics and data points" plus destination knowledge. UneeQ Synapse keeps answers safe, on-topic and private. The specific LLM is not disclosed.
ActionsRecommends destinations, builds itineraries, and since February 2025 supports AI booking on QVerse, app and website. Offers a human agent when it cannot help.
ChannelsQVerse (web 3D experience), mobile app, website; WhatsApp and Instagram presence reported; holograms at trade shows.
LanguagesLaunched in English; Arabic added in 2025 on website and app. Third-party case study reports six languages.
PersonaFull character with name, backstory and cultural identity; tone adapts (empathetic for complaints, enthusiastic for trip planning).
Reported scaleAbout 58,000 passenger queries per month (third-party case study, not an official Qatar figure).

What this means for you

Copy these ideas

  • Answers grounded in your own content, not the model's memory
  • Booking through real system tools, with confirmation
  • Human handoff when unsure
  • A defined persona and tone

Don't copy these first

  • Custom photoreal digital human (enterprise contract and budget)
  • Holograms and metaverse experiences
  • Many languages on day one

Key insight

Every layer that needs a GPU can be rented per use, or pushed to the user's own device. That is why a strong GPU computer is not a requirement. What you do need is a stable reservation API, good content, and careful rules around bookings and payment.

03 · Options without a GPU

Three ways to build the avatar

Option A · start here

3D avatar in the browser

A stylised 3D character (GLB file) is rendered on the user's device with Three.js. Open-source TalkingHead lip-syncs it from TTS audio with word or viseme timings.

  • Lowest running cost: you pay only LLM + speech APIs
  • Your server needs no GPU
  • Looks like a game/3D character, not a real person
  • Needs a decent phone/PC with WebGL
Option B · Sama-like

Rented photoreal streaming avatar

A vendor renders a realistic face on its GPUs and streams video to the user. You send it the voice audio.

  • Photoreal, closest to Sama
  • Pay per streamed minute
  • Works on weak phones (it is just video)
  • Vendor lock-in, data leaves to vendor
Option C · enterprise

Full digital-human platform

A vendor such as UneeQ builds a custom character, persona and conversation layer — what Qatar Airways did.

  • Highest quality and branding
  • Enterprise contract; pricing not public
  • Longest lead time
  • Only worth it after proven usage

04 · Requirements

What you need before and during the build

Hardware and hosting

  • No GPU. One cloud VM to start, e.g. 2–4 vCPU and 4–8 GB RAM for the orchestrator (load-test before launch; scale horizontally).
  • HTTPS domain; WebRTC needs TLS and, if self-hosting, TURN/UDP ports — a hosted transport (LiveKit Cloud or Daily) avoids that.
  • A database for conversations, knowledge chunks and audit logs (e.g. PostgreSQL with a vector extension).
  • Development laptop: any modern machine.

Reservation system

  • An API (REST/GraphQL) for: search availability, price, create hold, confirm, look up, modify, cancel.
  • A sandbox/test environment with fake data.
  • Holds that expire, and idempotent create calls (no double bookings on retry).
  • Payment through your existing checkout or a payment link — never typed card numbers in chat.
  • Customer identity: login or booking reference + last name / one-time code.

Accounts and services

  • LLM API with tool/function calling
  • Speech-to-text API (streaming)
  • Text-to-speech API, ideally returning word or viseme timings
  • Embeddings API for search
  • Option B only: streaming avatar vendor account
  • Error monitoring and log storage

Content and data

  • Policies: baggage, changes, refunds, cancellation, special assistance
  • FAQs and past support questions (anonymised)
  • Destination / product descriptions
  • Persona sheet: name, tone, words to avoid, escalation rules
  • A test set of 100–200 real questions with correct answers

People and skills

  • Backend developer (Python or Node; APIs; WebRTC basics)
  • Front-end developer (web/app, Three.js for Option A)
  • Content owner who maintains policies and answers
  • Designer/3D artist for the character (Option A) or persona video (Option B)
  • Support agent available for handoffs

Legal and trust

  • Clear notice that the user is talking to an AI (EU AI Act transparency rules and similar laws)
  • Consent and retention rules for voice recordings (GDPR or local law)
  • Data-processing agreements with each vendor; check data region
  • Payment data kept out of the AI pipeline (PCI DSS scope)
  • Licence for the avatar's likeness and voice

Example reservation tool

  "name": "search_availability",
  "description": "Find options. Read-only.",
  "parameters": {
    "type": "object",
    "properties": {
      "origin": {"type": "string"},
      "destination": {"type": "string"},
      "date": {"type": "string", "format":"date"},
      "guests": {"type": "integer", "minimum":1}

    "required": ["destination", "date", "guests"]

}

Golden rule for bookings

The AI proposes; the system and the customer decide. Read-only tools can run freely. Anything that holds, changes, cancels or charges shows a summary card and needs an explicit tap on "Confirm" in the UI — never only a spoken "yes" interpreted by the model. Prices always come from the reservation API, never from the model's text.

Sama-style assistant · R&D07
07 · Guide — phases 0 and 1
0

Discovery and decisions

  1. List the 10 most common customer questions and tasks (from support tickets, calls, chat logs). Pick 3–5 for the first release.
  2. Audit the reservation API: which operations exist, auth method, sandbox, rate limits, hold/expiry, idempotency. Write down gaps.
  3. Decide channels for launch (website first is easiest) and the first language.
  4. Choose Option A (browser 3D) or B (rented photoreal) for Phase 4 — it affects budget, not Phases 1–3.
  5. Define KPIs: answer accuracy, task completion, handoff rate, reply delay, cost per conversation, customer rating.
  6. Pick vendors for LLM, STT, TTS; sign data-processing agreements; confirm data region.

Exit: one-page scope, API gap list, KPI targets, budget ceiling, vendor shortlist.

1

Text assistant (read-only)

  1. Knowledge base. Collect policies and FAQs, split into short sections with titles and dates, create embeddings, store in the database. Every answer must cite its section internally.
  2. System prompt + persona. Name, tone, what it may and may not do, "say you don't know and offer a human" rule, AI disclosure line.
  3. Read-only tools. search_availability, get_price, get_booking (after identity check). Validate every argument on the server.
  4. Chat UI. Message list, typing indicator, result cards (options, prices from the API), "Talk to a person" button always visible.
  5. Logging. Store each turn, tool call, tool result and latency, with personal data masked.
  6. Evaluation. Run the 100–200 question test set automatically after every prompt or content change; review failures weekly.

Exit: test-set accuracy target met; zero invented prices; internal staff use it for a week.

Sama-style assistant · R&D08
08 · Guide — phases 2 and 3
2

Safe booking actions

  1. Add write tools: create_hold, modify_booking, cancel_booking. Each returns a summary, not a final result.
  2. Show a confirmation card (who, what, when, price from the API, cancellation terms). Only a tap on "Confirm" calls the confirm endpoint.
  3. Send payment to your existing checkout page or a payment link. The assistant never sees card data.
  4. Use idempotency keys on every write so network retries cannot double-book.
  5. Human handoff: pass the conversation summary and booking context to your support tool; tell the user the expected wait.
  6. Limits: max actions per session, block unusual patterns, require re-verification for cancellations.

Exit: end-to-end bookings in sandbox; no write without confirmation in tests; handoff tested with real agents.

3

Voice

  1. Add a real-time transport (LiveKit Cloud or Daily) and an orchestrator (Pipecat or LiveKit Agents) on your CPU server.
  2. Streaming STT with voice-activity detection; streaming TTS that starts speaking before the full answer is written.
  3. Barge-in: when the user talks, stop the voice immediately and listen.
  4. Keep spoken replies short (1–3 sentences) and put details — prices, times, lists — on cards on screen.
  5. Read back key booking details (dates, names, destination) before showing the confirm card.
  6. Measure delay from end of user speech to first audio; target about 1–1.5 s.

Exit: voice works on mid-range phones over mobile data; interruption works; delay within target.

Sama-style assistant · R&D09
09 · Guide — phases 4 to 6
4

Avatar and persona

Option A — browser 3D
  1. Create a character as a GLB with a Mixamo-compatible rig and ARKit + Oculus viseme blend shapes (TalkingHead requirement).
  2. Load it with TalkingHead / Three.js; lazy-load after the chat opens.
  3. Feed TTS audio plus word or viseme timings to speakAudio.
  4. Add idle, listening and thinking animations; keep a 2D fallback for weak devices.
Option B — rented photoreal
  1. Choose a stock avatar or commission a custom one with signed likeness consent.
  2. Use the vendor's LiveKit/Pipecat plugin so the vendor can be swapped later.
  3. Start the video session only when the user opens the assistant; end it on idle to stop billing.
  4. Fall back to voice-only if video fails.

Exit: lip-sync looks right to test users; avatar never delays the answer; cost per minute measured.

5

Languages and channels

  • Translate the knowledge base properly — do not rely on the model translating policies live.
  • Pick STT/TTS voices per language; check lip-sync support per language.
  • Add a channel such as WhatsApp (text first, voice notes later).
  • Run the test set per language.
6

Pilot, launch, operate

  • Pilot with 5–10% of visitors; compare against normal booking flow.
  • Dashboard: accuracy, completion, handoffs, delay, cost, ratings.
  • Weekly review of failed conversations; update content, not just prompts.
  • Incident plan: switch to text-only or off with one setting.

Cost formula to fill in after Phase 3

cost per conversation = STT minutes × rate + LLM tokens × rate + TTS characters × rate + avatar minutes × rate (Option B) + transport minutes × rate

Measure real values in the pilot. Option A removes the avatar term; turning video off on idle cuts Option B costs.
