01 — Logo

A sun settinginto the sea

The Sunseto mark is a low sun meeting three waves. It stands for the one promise we make: the afternoon’s light, kept for the evening.

Anatomy

Three parts, one line

  1. The markA sun disc sinking behind the horizon, clipped a sliver above the first wave, over three round-capped waves, each shorter than the one above. No rays.
  2. The wordmarksunseto, lowercase, in Instrument Sans SemiBold with each letter closed up by 6 units. Its baseline sits level with the first wave.

Which mark where

Flat for small, enamel for proud

The enamel mark lives inside the lockups and the app icon. At favicon size, in print and in single colours, use the flat SVG in one of the four colourways.

The seal signs things: the lockup, a postcard, the chart paper, the Step 1 heading. It is always pressed at a slight turn and multiplied into the paper. It never stands in for the logo.

The 11 px rule

Nothing under eleven

No text on a Sunseto page renders below 11px, on any screen. Fluid sizes are wrapped in max(11px, …), and text inside SVGs is sized for the box it is drawn into: the postmark’s 8.5 units become 11.1px.

The treatment

Ink lines, lit by the sky

Each engraving is white line on black, like a scratchboard. On the page it is laid over a painted sky with mix-blend-mode: screen, so the black disappears and the lines take the colour of the evening behind them.

On the site they are split into sky, far and near layers that drift apart as you scroll. Keep the sky in the upper third and the sun where the drawing puts it.

07 — Motion

Quick to arrive,slow to settle

Scroll tells the story; time only moves small things. Most things cover their distance early and settle slowly, the way a sheet of paper lands on a table.

流Nagare · flow
01

Scroll tells the story

Big moves are tied to scroll position, so the reader sets the pace and can always go back: the heading hand-off, the chart drawing in, the phone forming from its icon.

02

One thing at a time

A line of type, then the next, 90ms apart. A card settles, then its postmark comes down, then its seal. The eye always has one place to look.

03

Stillness is allowed

With reduced motion, reveals stop, ornaments hold still and the stamp simply appears. Nothing important depends on movement.

Durations

Between a breath and a sentence

UI answers in .3 to .6 seconds. Reveals take 1.2 seconds but are readable after the first third. Nothing loops fast; the slowest things, ornaments and the print’s clouds, move over seconds or minutes.

08 — Voice

Plain words,said warmly

We write in UK English, the way a good neighbour talks over the fence: short sentences, real numbers, no jargon. We promise one thing, the evening, and we say it simply.

声Koe · voice
Four habits
  1. Say the thingLead with what happens in someone’s home. “Your lamps stay on,” not “resilience”.
  2. Use real numbers¥9,800 a month. 4.8 kW. Eleven hours. Figures earn trust; adjectives don’t.
  3. Keep it warm, not cuteNeighbours, tea, the evening. No exclamation marks, no puns, no hype.
  4. UK spelling, alwaysColour, neighbour, centre, organise, metre, programme.

09 — Do and don’t

What wealways keep

Six rules that hold across the site, the app and anything else that carries the name. If a detail breaks one of them, the detail goes.

Don’t
01

No shines, glints, sheens, sweeps or light streaks

Polish comes from the drawing, the material and the light already in the scene, never from a highlight laid on top. If something feels flat, fix the lighting or the motion.

Screens, cards and panels are true rectangles with their edges parallel to the page. Depth belongs to floating decorative objects: the crane, the lantern, the app icon.
